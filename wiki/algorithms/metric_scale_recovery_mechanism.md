## Metric Scale Recovery Mechanism

The **Metric Scale Recovery Mechanism** is an algorithmic component that reconstructs metric-scale motion trajectories from monocular video, particularly from videos generated by simulation or rendering pipelines. It recovers metric scale from generated video frames, allowing quantitative evaluation of planning trajectories against SLAM references.

### Capabilities

- **Reconstruct motion from generated videos** – Given a video sequence (e.g., from a simulator or a rendered rollout), the mechanism recovers the 3D positions and orientations of the agent in metric units (meters/radians) rather than in arbitrary or normalized coordinates.
- **Enable metric-scale evaluation of planning performance** – By providing a metric-scale trajectory, the mechanism allows benchmarks to compute physically meaningful metrics (e.g., absolute trajectory error, success in distance‑based tasks) that would otherwise be impossible from a raw, scale‑ambiguous video. This enables fair comparison of planning policies on real-world-scale tasks.

### Dependencies

- SLAM – The mechanism often relies on monocular SLAM or structure‑from‑motion techniques to estimate relative camera poses, then applies a scale‑recovery step (e.g., using known object sizes, camera height, or ground plane offsets) to resolve the metric unknown.

### Use Cases

- Target-Bench – The mechanism is a core part of the Target-Bench evaluation framework. It extracts metric trajectories from videos generated by different policies (e.g., VLA models) and is responsible for the scale recovery that enables physically grounded comparison of planning outcomes.
