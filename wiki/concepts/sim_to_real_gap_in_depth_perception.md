## Sim-to-Real Gap in Depth Perception

### Overview

The **Sim-to-Real Gap in Depth Perception** refers to the systematic discrepancy between depth images generated by simulation environments and those obtained from real depth sensors (e.g., stereo cameras, LiDAR, structured light). This gap, caused by missing physical effects such as self-occlusion, sensor noise, specular reflections, and lighting variations, is a major obstacle to successful policy transfer from simulation to real hardware in robotics.

### Description

When training visual policies for manipulation or locomotion in simulation, agents learn to interpret depth maps that are clean, noise-free, and physically idealized. Real-world depth sensors, however, introduce artifacts:

- **Missing self-occlusion**: Simulated cameras often assume perfect visibility where real sensors may be blocked by the robot's own body.
- **Sensor noise**: Real depth data contains invalid pixels, Gaussian noise, and depth-dependent variance.
- **Lighting effects**: Specular surfaces, transparency, and ambient light interfere with stereo matching or time-of-flight measurements.

These differences cause a distribution shift that degrades policy performance upon deployment, as the agent encounters previously unseen input patterns. This gap is a subclass of the broader [[Sim-to-Real Transfer Problem|sim-to-real transfer problem]], specifically focusing on perceptual mismatch in depth modality.
